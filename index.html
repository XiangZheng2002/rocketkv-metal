Why decode slows down at long context

Every decode step attends to the whole KV cache. At 128K tokens the cache of a 3B model is 14.3 GB, so reading it costs more than reading the model weights, and per-token latency grows linearly with context. RocketKV caps how much of the cache each step touches.

Method: two stages, both on the GPU

Stage 1 permanently evicts unimportant prompt tokens once, right after prefill. Stage 2 runs at every decode step in every layer and attends to only k of the remaining tokens. Use the controls to step through each animation.

Kernel engineering: 9 dispatches → 2

Profiling showed Stage 2 was bound by the serial latency of its stages and the barrier after each kernel, not by memory bandwidth. The fix was to fuse the pipeline into two wide kernels where the last threadgroup to finish does the global step.

What made the difference
  • Split-K exact attention with online softmax; the 3 query heads of a GQA group share every K/V read.
  • Last-arriving threadgroup (atomic ticket) runs softmax + top-k or the final merge; cross-threadgroup data uses coherent(device) buffers with acquire/release fences.
  • 8-bit radix select with simdgroup prefix sums replaces a bitonic sort; chunks are counted by token count.
  • Incremental summaries: chunk min/max persist across steps, so only new tokens cost work.
  • A/B discipline: one change at a time, interleaved runs; changes under 2% or with long-input numerical drift were reverted (see the log).

Takeaways and limits

Wins at long context

Stage 1+2 decode is flat at ≈7.2 ms/token. It beats full KV from 32K (1.27×) up to 128K (2.91×). Below 16K the fixed scoring cost makes it slower than full KV (0.71–0.91×).

Prefill still dominates end-to-end

RocketKV only speeds up decode. At 128K prefill takes 110 s, so end-to-end time for 128 output tokens is unchanged (1.01×); eviction itself adds just 40 ms.

Accuracy depends on the task

At budget 1024, LongBench drops 1.4 points and RULER without multi-key retrieval stays within noise up to 64K. niah_multikey_2 drops sharply (e.g. 70 → 30 at 64K) because Stage 1 evicts keys that are needed later.

Correctness checks

  • CPU reference implementation (src/rocket.cpp) is the test oracle for budget derivation, Stage 1 selection and HSA output.
  • Metal kernel tests: selection recall ≥ 0.98 vs. the CPU reference; attention output vs. fp64 attention over the same selection; split-boundary cases (one caught a NaN bug on empty blocks).
  • On 60 RULER samples at 2K/4K/8K, CPU and Metal versions of Stage 1 and Stage 1+2 produce 100% identical generations.
  • Runtime guards: every layer runs HSA exactly once per step; Metal Stage 1 copies 0 host bytes; every output is finite. UBSan and Metal shader validation in CI-style test runs.
